Solar in Oakland.

I grew up here and I've put solar on more than two hundred East Bay homes. Oakland isn't one solar market — the flatlands and the hills are genuinely different problems.

Last updated: September 16, 2026

Two Oaklands, two projects

A bungalow off High Street and a house on Skyline are both Oakland addresses and almost nothing about their solar projects is the same.

The flatlands

Mostly Craftsman bungalows, Victorians, and post-war stucco. Good sun, few hills in the way, and generally simpler roofs — though Victorians bring steep pitches and multiple small planes that raise labor. The recurring issue down here is electrical: a great many of these houses still have 100-amp service, and Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels turn up constantly. That's usually the first thing I look at, and it's covered in the panel guide.

The hills

Montclair, Rockridge, Oakmore, Skyline. Larger homes, larger bills, and two complications: mature redwoods and oaks that shade roofs badly, and terrain that puts a hillside between your array and the afternoon sun. Shade here is not a detail — it decides the design. Module-level electronics are usually right, and a production estimate that doesn't model your specific canopy honestly isn't worth much.

The hills are also largely within a high fire-threat district. That matters for two reasons: PSPS shutoffs are a real part of life, which changes what backup is worth to you, and homes in those districts are among the few that can still reach the meaningful tiers of California's battery rebate program. More in do I need a battery.

Does it still pencil out here?

Oakland is PG&E territory, so NEM 3 applies in full: you'll export at a few cents and reimport at north of thirty-five. For most Oakland homes that means solar works when it's paired with storage and when the bill is big enough to justify both. The full reasoning, including what the expired federal credit did to the math in 2026, is in is solar still worth it under NEM 3.

I tell people no fairly often — a small bill, heavy redwood shade, a roof with three years left. That's not modesty, it's the job. If it doesn't work for your house I'd rather you hear it from me for free than find out after financing it.

Roofs first

Oakland's housing stock is old, and a good share of the roofs I look at are within a few years of needing replacement. Putting an array on one of those means paying to remove and reinstall it later. If your roof is near the end, I will say so, and I can point you at roofers I'd use on my own house and help sequence the two jobs so they don't fight each other.

Permits and PG&E

Permitting runs through the City of Oakland and interconnection through PG&E. Most projects land in the 30-to-45-day range from signing to permission to operate, with the variance mostly in plan review and in whether a service upgrade is involved. See the timeline guide for where the weeks actually go. I handle the submissions either way.

Why local matters here

Not as a slogan. It's that I know which blocks have the panel problem, which hill streets lose the sun at three in the afternoon in December, and which roofs under those redwoods will never make what a national sales tool predicts from a satellite image. That's the difference between a design that works and a proposal that models beautifully.

I also live here. If something goes wrong in year six, I'm still fifteen minutes away.
