Solar in San Leandro.

San Leandro is, honestly, one of the easier places in the East Bay to put solar on a house — and that shows up in the price.

Last updated: September 16, 2026

Why projects here tend to go smoothly

A lot of San Leandro is post-war single-story ranch: composition shingle, shallow pitch, one or two large uninterrupted roof planes, and a lot fewer mature trees than Rockridge or the Berkeley hills.

That combination is the cheap case for solar, for real reasons rather than marketing ones:

  • Walkable pitch means faster, safer labor and no fall protection premium.
  • Large single planes mean less racking, fewer roof penetrations, and fewer hours than the same wattage split across six small facets.
  • Less shade means a more predictable production estimate and sometimes a simpler inverter choice.
  • Newer service than the pre-war stock further north — though plenty of 100-amp panels are still out here, so it's worth checking.

When I say the per-watt price is lower in San Leandro than in Piedmont, this is why. It isn't a different rate card; it's a different roof. The full list of what moves a quote is in what solar actually costs.

The economics, honestly

San Leandro is PG&E territory, so NEM 3 governs: exports at a few cents, imports at retail. An easy roof lowers the cost side, but it doesn't change the fundamental question, which is how much power you use and when.

If your bill is modest and your usage is mostly in the evening, a solar-only system will disappoint you no matter how cheap the roof was to work on. If you're running AC, an EV, or a pool, the numbers here tend to be good — a straightforward roof and a real load is the best combination there is. See is solar still worth it under NEM 3 and do I need a battery.

What I still check

  • Roof age. Much of this stock is on its second or third roof. If yours is near the end, that job goes first.
  • Panel capacity. 100-amp service is common and may need addressing before a battery or a charger.
  • Additions. Ranch houses get added onto, and the roof over an addition isn't always framed to the same standard. Worth knowing before loading it.
