Comparing solar companies in Oakland? Start here.

There is no single best solar company in Oakland, only the best fit for your roof, your panel and your budget. Here is how I compare them, and what to ask before you sign.

Last updated: October 10, 2026

Who is writing this. I'm Isaac Roth, an independent solar consultant in Oakland. I don't work for an installer and I'm not paid by one to send you their way, so this guide is about how to choose, not a ranking with a winner already picked.

Why "best solar company in Oakland" is the wrong question

Search for it and you'll find a lot of ranked lists. A list can't see your roof. The company that is right for a simple, south-facing ranch in San Leandro may be wrong for a three-story Victorian in the Oakland flats with a 100-amp panel and a lot of shade. Fit matters more than rank.

The kinds of solar companies you'll meet

  • National installers. Big marketing budgets, standard packages, and often a sales team separate from the crews. Pricing can be competitive on simple roofs, and service after the sale depends on how they staff the Bay Area.
  • Local installers. Smaller, usually with their own crews and a local reputation to protect. Quality varies a lot in both directions, so check recent work in your neighborhood.
  • Door-to-door and lead-generation sellers. Often the company that knocks isn't the company that installs. Ask who will actually be on your roof.
  • Independent consultants. That's me. We don't install; we help you understand the options and compare quotes from installers.

How to compare two quotes

The goal is to compare like for like. Before you look at the price, make sure both quotes describe the same job:

  • The same system size in kilowatts, designed to cover the same share of your usage.
  • Specific equipment: the manufacturer and model of the panels, inverter and battery, not "premium panels".
  • The total installed price in dollars, plus the cost per watt. A monthly payment alone hides both. I break this down in what solar costs in Oakland.
  • Warranties listed separately: panels, inverter, battery and workmanship are different promises from different parties, for different lengths of time.
  • Purchase, loan or lease. The same panels can cost very different amounts over their life depending on how you pay. See lease, loan or buy.

Questions to ask every company

  • Who will install this: your own crew, or a subcontractor?
  • Who handles the permit and the PG&E application, and what happens if it stalls? (How long it takes.)
  • Is my main electrical panel good enough, or does it need work first? Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels have to be replaced before solar.
  • Does the savings estimate account for NEM 3 and for the 2026 end of the homeowner federal tax credit? (The NEM 3 guide.)
  • Do I need a battery, and why? (Do I need a battery?)
  • Who do I call, and how fast, when something needs service in year five?

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign the same day, or a price that's "only good tonight".
  • A proposal that still shows the 30% federal credit for a system you buy in 2026.
  • A "$0 down" offer where you can't see the price without the financing fees.
  • A vague equipment list, or no production estimate with the shade assumptions behind it.
  • Reluctance to put the warranty terms in writing.

Oakland-specific things worth knowing

  • Utility. Most of Oakland is served by PG&E, so NEM 3 rules apply. Alameda is different; it's served by Alameda Municipal Power (solar in Alameda).
  • Old housing stock. Many Oakland homes have older panels and complicated roofs, which is where quotes tend to diverge.
  • Hills versus flats. Shade, access and roof pitch change the design and the price. More in solar in Oakland.

Where I fit in

I look at your roof, your panel and your bill, then review the quotes you've collected or help you get comparable ones from trusted local installers. If the fair quote is from someone I've never worked with, I'll say so.
