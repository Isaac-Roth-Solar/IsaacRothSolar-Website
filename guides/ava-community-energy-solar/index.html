Ava Community Energy and solar: what changes on your bill.

If you live in Oakland, Berkeley, Piedmont or San Leandro, your electricity generation comes from Ava by default, not PG&E, and that changes how solar credits work. Here is what to know before you sign a solar contract.

Last updated: October 30, 2026

Read this first. The rates and dates below come from Ava's Solar Billing Plan page and Ava's net energy metering page as I read them in October 2026. Credit values change, so confirm the current figures with Ava before you rely on any number here.

What Ava is, and where it applies

Ava Community Energy, formerly East Bay Community Energy, buys the electricity that is delivered to homes across most of Alameda County. Its service area includes Oakland, Berkeley, Piedmont, San Leandro, Emeryville, Albany, Hayward, Fremont and others. Ava's service map excludes the island of Alameda, which has its own story; see solar in Alameda.

The practical effect is that your solar system sits between two organizations: Ava for the electricity you generate and buy, and PG&E for delivery. That is why solar customers in Ava territory see two separate annual true-ups.

Which solar billing you are on

Ava puts solar customers into one of two plans, based on when the system was approved:

  • Net energy metering (NEM). Systems whose interconnection application was submitted before April 15, 2023. These customers are guaranteed 20 years on NEM.
  • The Solar Billing Plan (SBP). Systems applied for on or after that date, systems that finish their 20 NEM years, and systems whose capacity grows by 1 kW or 10%. This is the Ava version of what most people call NEM 3. More on NEM 3 here.

A new system you install now will be on the Solar Billing Plan.

How the Solar Billing Plan pays you for exports

  • Credits vary by hour and day. Exports earn Energy Export Credits based on what the electricity is worth when it goes to the grid. Credits apply at the end of each monthly cycle and excess credits roll over.
  • Ava bonus for evening exports. Ava lists an extra $0.025 per kWh for exports between 3 and 8 pm, for customers who are not on the CARE or FERA assistance programs.
  • Ava bonus for CARE and FERA customers. An extra $0.01 per kWh on exports.
  • An export bonus that steps down by year. Ava lists an Energy Export Bonus Credit for new residential systems, locked in for nine years. For a residential system interconnected in 2026 it is $0.009 per kWh, falling to $0.004 in 2027. Income-qualified customers get a higher amount.
  • The E-Elec rate plan. Ava says residential Solar Billing Plan customers must be on the E-Elec rate plan. Ask whether your installer's savings estimate uses it.

Because of the evening bonus, battery sizing is worth modeling for Ava customers. A system that can export during the 3 to 8 pm window is the kind of system that bonus rewards. Do you need a battery?

The two true-ups, and what is paid out

Every solar customer in Ava territory gets two annual reconciliations: one for Ava generation charges and one for PG&E delivery charges. They can fall in different months, which surprises people the first year.

  • Ava's true-up is each April. Surplus generation is paid at the Net Surplus Compensation rate. Ava describes that rate as typically about 2 to 4 cents per kWh.
  • Small balances stay on your bill. Under $100 is applied as a bill credit. Over $100 is paid through your preferred payment method, usually in June or July, by a third-party vendor, Choice Digital.

Bright Choice or Renewable 100

Ava offers two main plans. The retail rate used for your monthly solar credits and charges depends on your rate schedule and your plan, either Bright Choice or Renewable 100. The surplus payout rate does not change with the plan. The plan you start on depends on where you live. Ava's default table lists Bright Choice for Oakland, and Renewable 100 for residential accounts in Berkeley, Piedmont and San Leandro. Check which one is on your account, because a solar quote that assumes the wrong plan will misstate your savings.

What to ask your installer

  • Is the savings estimate built for Ava territory, or for PG&E generation?
  • Which plan, Bright Choice or Renewable 100, is it based on?
  • Does it count the 3 to 8 pm bonus, and on what battery size?
  • Does it assume the E-Elec rate?
  • Does the proposal say anything about the two true-ups?

A proposal that never mentions Ava probably wasn't built for your bill. How to compare solar companies in Oakland covers the rest of what to check.
